## Writing methods and research

Autoregressive language models generate text from learned distributions conditioned on preceding text. Examples supplied in a prompt can influence the response without retraining the model. This explains one mechanism for imitation, rather than establishing that an output reproduces an individual's experience. [Brown et al., Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165).

Flower and Hayes describe human composition as goal-directed planning, putting ideas into language and reviewing, with those activities recurring throughout a draft. Audience, remembered knowledge and the developing text influence the writer's decisions. This is a foundational cognitive model, not a claim that all people follow one fixed sequence. [A Cognitive Process Theory of Writing (1981)](https://doi.org/10.58680/ccc198115885).

Reinhart and colleagues found systematic grammatical and rhetorical differences across human writing, GPT-4o and Llama 3 variants, including stronger differences for instruction-tuned models. Their results support attention to structural variation rather than a universal vocabulary blacklist. [PNAS study, revised 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16107).

Bagdasarov and Alves found greater human syntactic variation, but greater model lexical variation, in their scientific-text comparison. More unusual synonyms alone therefore aren't a reliable recipe for a human voice. The finding belongs to that corpus and those models. [LM4DH paper (2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.lm4dh-1.4/).

A 2026 preprint comparing eleven models across eight genres reports that genre has a stronger stylistic influence than human/model source, and that some differences persist under prompting. This cautions against treating “write like a human” as a complete editing method. [Rallapalli et al. (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14111).

NN/g's framework treats tone through humor, formality, respectfulness and enthusiasm. It offers a practical way to choose a consistent web voice while adjusting it to the subject. [The Four Dimensions of Tone of Voice](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/).

## Editorial application

The voice is inferred from Jeremy's direct project instructions: clear about the desired result, attentive to visuals and story continuity, and specific when asking for corrections. No independent long-form writing sample was supplied, so this is an informed editorial interpretation rather than a verified replica of his prose.

The logs use first-person ownership, ordinary verbs, contractions where natural, concrete changes and reasons, and varied paragraph lengths. Technical details explain the effect on visitors or deployment. Each entry has its own topic; paragraphs aren't forced into a repeated problem/solution/result template.

Unsupported anecdotes, remembered conversations, coffee scenes, audience reactions and performance measurements were removed. A source-code change is described as a change, not proof of a benchmark or of universal device compatibility. Historical plans are separated from completed work. Character details follow the approved eleven-fragment canon, including the Walkers' alternate-self relationship and Unknown's concealed identity.

Each article ends with relevant GitHub commits and source-file links pinned to the version discussed. Shared-commit work is disclosed in entries 09 and 10; the split describes two aspects of one release. Previous prose remains recoverable in Git history.

These are editorial choices drawn from the research and project evidence. They aren't an AI-detector guarantee or a claim that assisted writing has become unassisted writing.
